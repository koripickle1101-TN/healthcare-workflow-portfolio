# Implementation Safety Stop/Go Decision Framework

**Healthcare Operations Intelligence Engine™**  
**Created by Kori Pickle | BSHA Candidate, University of Phoenix**

> **Student-developed, simulated, educational, no-PHI healthcare operations portfolio project.** This is a proposed operational review framework, not a hospital-approved policy, clinical protocol, legal opinion, or validated patient-safety intervention.

## Executive question

**Where did the workflow first lose control?**

A hospital implements a hypothetical medication administration workflow. During rollout, staff report workarounds, delayed steps, and safety concerns. The organizational risk is not only the observed problem: it is allowing rollout to continue without defined response criteria, a responsible decision-maker, documented containment, and evidence supporting resumption.

## Distinct portfolio contribution

The existing near-miss reporting case study addresses **detecting and reporting safety signals**. This framework addresses the next decision: **when those signals should stop or change an implementation**. It develops practical project-support capabilities: monitoring, handoff documentation, escalation, decision logging, and corrective-action follow-through.

## Simulated workflow

1. **Prepare:** Define the affected service, staff training expectations, baseline measures, known risks, and designated clinical safety contacts.
2. **Observe:** Capture safety signals through authorized reporting mechanisms and aggregate performance measures.
3. **Triage:** Distinguish an urgent potential harm concern from a trend requiring additional review. Do not assume fewer reported incidents means safer care.
4. **Decide:** Route findings to the proper clinical, patient safety, implementation, and executive decision owners.
5. **Contain:** Follow applicable organizational emergency stop policies, protect patients, and document temporary safeguards.
6. **Correct:** Assign owners, due dates, and effectiveness tests.
7. **Resume or revise:** Require documented review and authorized sign-off before restarting an affected workflow.
8. **Monitor:** Reassess performance after resumption and escalate recurring concerns.

## Student-designed decision matrix (illustrative; NOT clinical thresholds)

| Simulated signal | Proposed response | Decision/approval boundary | Evidence to retain |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Serious harm or an immediately credible danger potentially linked to rollout | Urgent safety escalation; follow organization policy for immediate containment or stopping affected work | Authorized clinical safety personnel and organizational emergency-stop process; implementation team does not override clinical safety obligations | Incident time, relevant confirmed facts, immediate protective actions, notification log |
| Multiple near misses, workarounds, or worsening trends | Targeted review, additional controls, and possible pause of affected portion pending review | Clinical/patient safety leadership with implementation owner under defined governance | Trend denominators, severity, observation period, staff feedback, decision record |
| Isolated workflow issue without identified immediate harm | Correct the defect and intensify monitoring as directed | Operational/project owner within delegated authority, with safety escalation where warranted | Issue log, fix owner, validation result |
| Evidence shows corrective controls are effective | Consider controlled resumption and enhanced surveillance | Named authorized decision-makers; document agreed approval and monitoring plan | Competency checks, testing evidence, written approval, post-restart review plan |

**Important:** The matrix is an educational example, not a validated scoring tool or universal pause criterion. Actual hospitals must use local policy, clinical governance, approved risk methods, and applicable requirements. Clinical staff should not delay necessary emergency safety measures while waiting for administrative sign-off.

## Decision-rights and escalation map

| Role | Proposed responsibility |
| --- | --- |
| Frontline staff | Report concerns, follow immediate safety procedures, record observable facts |
| Clinical supervisor / authorized clinical leader | Assess immediate patient danger, direct clinical safeguards within authority |
| Patient safety / risk management | Review signals, coordinate investigation and escalation, track risk decisions |
| Implementation / operations lead | Identify affected rollout steps, manage issue and remediation logs, coordinate testing |
| Designated executive / governance body | Resolve substantial organizational trade-offs within formal authority; ensure clinical safeguards are not bypassed |
| Legal / compliance | Advise on applicable obligations, confidentiality, preservation, and reporting; not substitute for clinical decision-making |

## Illustrative KPIs

- **Escalation timeliness:** qualifying signals routed within the timeframe defined by local policy / total qualifying signals.
- **Overdue corrective actions:** actions past assigned due date / all open actions.
- **Validation completion:** corrections with documented effectiveness assessment / corrections due for assessment.
- **Workflow workaround rate:** observed workaround events / observed workflow opportunities (use consistent sampling).
- **Near-miss reporting context:** reporting counts interpreted alongside severity, staff feedback, and independent checks, not as a simple lower-is-better target.
- **Post-resumption recurrence:** repeated targeted failure signals within a documented observation period, with consistent event definitions and denominators.

No values or hospital outcomes are claimed. A future spreadsheet dashboard could use synthetic observations to demonstrate these calculations.

## Companion scenario: patient harm, transparency, and accountability

If the rollout contributes to serious patient harm, the decision system must connect **immediate patient protection → preservation of factual evidence → authorized clinical communication and follow-up → investigation of upstream conditions → corrective action → effectiveness verification**. Legal, privacy, reporting, and peer-review protections require qualified review; staff must not treat confidentiality as permission to give misleading accounts or conceal required facts.

This companion illustrates why identifying the *first missed control* matters more than blaming only the final worker in a failure chain. It does **not** assert actual findings, legal duties in a particular state, or access to protected records.
